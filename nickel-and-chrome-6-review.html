BATHLAVISH builds the Nickel and Chrome 6 around a 304 stainless steel panel reinforced to resist the dents and corrosion that cheaper shower panels develop within a year or two. Behind the nickel and chrome finish sits a brass mixing valve and diverter, the same basic hardware you would find in a mid-range single shower valve, just routed through six rotary switches instead of one lever. Those switches split the water flow into six programs: a pulsating massage mode, a gentle mist setting, a full-body mode that runs the rainfall head and side jets together, a quick handheld spray, a waterfall tub fill, and a water-saving single stream for when you do not need the whole panel running. The wraparound shape puts the rain shower, four massage jets, handheld spray, and tub spout into a single column, so you are not bolting separate fixtures to the wall one at a time.

At $237.49, the Nickel and Chrome 6 sits near the top of what we found in this nickel and chrome 6 review, roughly $7 to $8 above the RUMOSE and ELLO&ALLO panels covered below. That gap buys brass instead of the mixed brass-and-PVC waterways some competitors use, plus a sixth mode the five-function alternatives do not offer. It makes the most sense if more than one person showers in the household and you want distinct modes for a quick morning rinse versus an evening soak, rather than one generic rainfall setting. If you only ever use a shower panel on one setting, the extra modes and the price premium are harder to justify, and a simpler panel from RUMOSE or ELLO&ALLO will cover the basics for less.

Who Is It For?

The Nickel and Chrome 6 fits households that actually use a shower panel's full range, not just the rainfall head. If you want a dedicated mist setting for a quick cool-down, a full-body mode for a longer soak, and a separate tub-fill function, the six-program layout earns its price. It is a weaker fit if you live alone and tend to favor one shower mode out of habit, or if you are retrofitting a small bathroom where the simpler RUMOSE or ELLO&ALLO panels already cover what you need for less money. Renters who might move within a year or two should also weigh the brass internals against the price difference, since the durability advantage of the Nickel and Chrome 6 matters most over several years of daily use rather than a single lease term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BATHLAVISH Nickel and Chrome 6 shower panel easy to install?

Most wall-mounted shower panels, including this one, bolt onto existing plumbing lines with a handful of wall anchors rather than requiring new rough-in work. The six-function body routes the rain shower, four body jets, and handheld spray through a single waterway, so check the manufacturer's rough-in dimensions against your bathroom before you buy.

What spa modes does the Nickel and Chrome 6 shower panel offer?

The panel switches between six settings: a pulsating massage mode, a gentle mist mode, a full-body mode that runs the rainfall head and side jets together, a quick handheld spray, a waterfall tub fill, and a water-saving single stream. Rotary switches built into the panel face select the mode.

Is the Nickel and Chrome 6 worth the price compared to other shower panels?

At $237.49, it costs about $7 to $8 more than the RUMOSE and ELLO&ALLO alternatives covered in this nickel and chrome 6 review. The difference buys a 304 stainless steel body and a brass mixing valve, which matters if you want the panel to outlast a decade of daily use rather than just the warranty period.

Final Verdict

Our nickel and chrome 6 review lands on the BATHLAVISH panel as the stronger buy for most multi-person households, even at $237.49. The combination of a brass mixing valve, a 304 stainless steel body, and six distinct spa modes outweighs the premium over the RUMOSE and ELLO&ALLO alternatives, especially if you plan to keep the panel installed for more than a couple of years.
