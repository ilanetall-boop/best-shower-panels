Things to Know Before You Buy

  • A shower pan is the waterproof floor base under your tile. The systems below are the wall-mounted panel towers you pair with it, not the pan itself, so plan the two purchases separately.
  • These panels mount to a wall stud or blocking behind the tile, not the tile surface. Set your blocking before the tile goes up if you're renovating from scratch.
  • Material decides lifespan, not mode count. Stainless steel frames and brass valves resist corrosion far longer than the plastic components on some budget towers, based on the verified buyer feedback we researched.
  • Prices in this comparison run from $118.90 to $369.00, and the jump in price doesn't always buy more function, mostly more massage jets.
  • Most of these systems take a half day of plumbing work and usually need two people, so budget install time before you commit to a specific pick.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best shower pan system for tile showers right now?

Based on the frame material, jet layout, and verified owner reviews we compared, the VEVOR Shower Panel System 5 is the best shower pan system for tile installs for most bathrooms. It pairs a stainless steel frame with five shower modes at a price under $120. Buyers who want more massage jets should look at the VEVOR Shower Panel Tower System or the Blue Ocean 52-inch Aluminum SPA392M instead.

Can I mount a shower panel system directly onto tile?

Yes, these systems mount to a wall stud or blocking behind the tile rather than to the tile surface itself, so plan the blocking placement before the tile goes up. Reviewers across the seven systems we compared note that a secure wall anchor matters more for long-term stability than the panel's material.

Do more shower modes mean better water pressure?

Not necessarily. Reviewers across the seven systems we compared report that water pressure depends more on your home's plumbing than on the number of printed modes, and running more jets at once can reduce flow per outlet. Frame material, like the stainless steel and brass valve construction used in several picks here, tends to affect long-term performance more than mode count does.
