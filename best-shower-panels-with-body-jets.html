Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Body jets need real water pressure to work. Most body jet towers want at least 40 PSI to keep every jet firing at once; on weak home plumbing the side jets can feel more like a trickle than a massage.
  • More jets is not automatically better. A panel with six or eight body jets spreads the same water pressure across more outlets, so a tower with fewer, larger jets can actually feel stronger on average plumbing.
  • Stainless steel holds up better than painted aluminum. A humid shower stall is hard on cheap finishes, and a stainless or stainless-clad body resists rust and pitting far longer than a painted shell.
  • Installation replaces your existing valve, not just the showerhead. Plan for a wall-mounted tower that connects to your hot and cold lines, which usually means a more involved install than swapping a showerhead.
  • LED displays and mood lighting do not affect jet pressure. A lit temperature screen is a nice-to-have, but it has no bearing on how hard the body jets actually spray, so judge a panel on its plumbing first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water pressure do I need for a shower panel with body jets?

Plan on at least 40 PSI to run a rainfall head and multiple body jets at full strength together. Most residential plumbing falls in the 40 to 60 PSI range, which is enough for the panels in this comparison, but on weaker lines the body jets can feel more like a trickle than a spray. If your home's pressure is on the low end, look for a tower with fewer, larger jets rather than one that spreads the same flow across more outlets.

Are stainless steel shower panels worth the extra cost over aluminum?

For most households, yes. A humid shower stall is hard on cheap finishes, and stainless steel resists the rust spots and pitting that show up on painted aluminum bodies within a year or two of daily use. If you shower daily and plan to keep the panel installed for several years, the modest price premium on a tower like the BWE Stainless Steel Shower Panel pays off in longevity.

Do more body jets always mean a stronger shower?

No. Adding more jets spreads the same water pressure across more outlets, so a panel with six or eight jets is not automatically stronger than one with fewer, larger jets on the same plumbing. Check what verified owners report about pressure rather than assuming a higher jet count wins, and match the tower to your home's actual water pressure.
