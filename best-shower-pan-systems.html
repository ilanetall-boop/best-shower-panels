Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Most panel-style shower pan systems connect to your existing hot and cold supply lines, so you rarely need to open a wall to install one.
  • Body massage jets and multi-mode shower heads need at least 40 psi of static water pressure to perform the way the listing photos suggest.
  • Stainless steel and anodized aluminum panels resist the rust spots that chrome-plated plastic units develop within a year or two.
  • Prices in this guide run from $98.09 to $369.00, and the jump in cost usually buys more jets, a bigger panel, or an LED display rather than better core plumbing.
  • LED and digital display models draw power, so check whether your bathroom has a GFCI outlet within reach before you buy one.

Finding the best shower pan systems starts with the panel tower itself, since most people shopping this category already have a shower pan in place and want to upgrade what sprays out of the wall above it. We compared seven multi-function shower panel towers ranging from $98.09 to $369.00, looking at jet count, material, and what each brand actually includes in the box. Our top pick, the VEVOR Shower Panel System 5, pairs a rainfall shower head with two body massage jets for $98.09, which makes it the easiest way to add real spray options without touching the plumbing behind your existing shower pan.

Every panel here mounts flush to the wall and routes hot and cold water through a single mixer valve, then splits it between a fixed rain head, a handheld wand, and, on most models, a row of adjustable body jets. Some add LED lighting that changes color with water temperature, and a few include a shelf or holder for shampoo bottles. None of that changes how the shower pan itself drains, but it does change how much water pressure and how many spray patterns you get standing in the stall.

I started with panel towers priced between $90 and $400, since that range covers most homeowners replacing a single shower head with a multi-function system rather than doing a full remodel. From there I dropped any panel without a clearly listed material, because chrome-finished plastic bodies tend to crack or discolor faster than stainless steel or aluminum. I also gave weight to panels with more than one spray mode beyond the standard rain head, since a single fixed head rarely justifies the price jump over a basic shower head swap. The seven best shower pan systems that made the cut all list their jet count or spray modes clearly and come from brands with enough sales history to have a real base of owner reviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a shower pan and a shower panel system?

A shower pan is the waterproof base or floor of your shower stall. A shower panel system is the wall-mounted tower that controls water flow, typically combining a rain head, handheld wand, and body jets. The best shower pan systems in this guide install onto your existing pan and plumbing, so you don't have to replace the pan itself.

Do shower pan systems require professional installation?

Most panels in this guide connect to your existing hot and cold supply lines and don't require new plumbing, so a handy homeowner can often install one in an afternoon. Models with more jets or LED lighting add extra connections, and if your water pressure or wall layout is unusual, have a plumber check the install first.

How much water pressure do I need for a multi-jet shower panel?

Most panels with three or more body jets perform best with at least 40 psi of static water pressure. Below that, you'll notice weaker spray from the jets even though the rain head and handheld wand still work fine, so check your home's pressure before buying a panel with a high jet count.
